A JUDGE MOSTLY ASKS

“Does this match what I get?”

It re-derives the answer, out loud when thinking, silently when not. Belief predicts where that lands, so it predicts the verdict.

INSIDE

Solving and judging encode “right” differently; at the verdict, a match reads as “right”.

THE 18% FLOOR

Comes from the judge frame: judging, it follows the candidate’s path. Solving (2%) or committing first (1%) removes it.

For a jury: when a judge strongly believes an answer, asking it to check harder won't help, since its own derivation agrees. A model with different beliefs is needed (still to test). Committing the judge's own answer first removes the low-belief floor, but also rejects more right answers it doesn't reach.
