About 1 minute. Everything so far was found and tested on the same data, and flexible analysis choices can produce false findings. The benchmarks have no unused questions, so I rebuilt JudgeBench’s recipe on new ones. From MMLU-Pro questions JudgeBench doesn’t contain, Nemotron-3.5-Super wrote six answers each; the 1,336 questions with at least one right and one wrong answer became pairs. Four judges from three other families (Qwen3.6-35B, Qwen3.5-9B, DeepSeek, Kimi) picked the better response and answered each question eight times on their own. The plan, analyses and sample size were written down before collecting data (not registered with a third party). Result: the wrong response is picked 19% of the time at 0/7 and 63% at 7/7; the pre-specified slope is +47 points, and every judge’s slope is above zero. Two things were added after seeing the results, and I label them so: a lenient answer parser (the strict one dropped 22% of judgments, unequally across judges) and an audit of every wrong response. Both leave the slope at +43 to +48. Same candidate, different judges: +19, so here the judge-specific part is clear.
