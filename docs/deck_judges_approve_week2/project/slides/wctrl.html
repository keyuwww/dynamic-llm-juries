About 1 minute 15. Four things that could fake this pattern, each with a test. One, difficulty: on hard questions a judge’s answers scatter and it may also be lenient. So compare only wrong answers to the same question graded by the same judge: the one it believes more is approved more, +47 points. That is clear for the Qwens and both Nemotrons, uncertain for DeepSeek, and absent for Kimi. Two, some wrong answers may fool every model: set the judge’s own belief against the average belief of judges from other families in the same answer. Both predict approval, own +25, others +23, so the effect is partly shared and partly the judge’s own; on the new multiple-choice data the judge’s own part is bigger, +34. Three, maybe one sampled answer is enough: fitted together for the Qwens, belief carries +43 and the single sample +2, and the other families agree. Even so, belief predicts individual verdicts only modestly (AUROC about 0.73). Four, maybe the judge just didn’t think: with thinking on, the Qwens get much stricter on answers they don’t believe, 17 → 3% at 0/7, and the rise shows no clear change, +50 against +43. Caveat: this is all observational; nothing here changes a judge’s belief directly.
