About 45 seconds. I want to be upfront about what moved. One: the headline Qwen numbers drop from 18 → 74% to 17 → 59% once disputed labels are removed. Two: last week I said that inside the model, believing an answer looks like the answer being correct, more so when judging than when solving. That does not hold up. When I shuffle the belief labels within each question, most of the overlap stays, so it came from which questions the believed answers come from, not from belief. The cleaner test compares two wrong answers to the same question and finds only small effects, 0.56 to 0.64 of pairs, where 0.5 means nothing; choosing the layer without the test questions gives about the same. Three: with thinking on, transcripts still show a derivation ending at the approved answer in 93% of approvals (25% with thinking off), but that shows what the judge wrote, not how it decided. Four: the expert-reference gain is +12.3 rather than +15 on re-checked labels. What still stands: committing first fixes errors on answers the judge doesn’t believe, and a second judge from another family doesn’t fix the ones it does.
