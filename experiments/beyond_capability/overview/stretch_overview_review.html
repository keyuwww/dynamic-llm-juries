Small LLM judges · inference-time edit · Nov 16, 2026

Small judges know more than they say. Stretch it out of them.

A judge reads a question and one answer, then says CORRECT or INCORRECT. Inside the model there is a direction that tracks whether the answer is actually right, and it often ranks answers better than the verdict does. We find that direction, pick the layer the verdict actually reads, and stretch answers apart along it.

1 · Find the direction

At each layer: average state of right answers minus wrong ones, per question. Learned on half the questions, tested on the other half.

2 · Check the gap

How much better does the direction rank answers than the verdict? Stop if 5 points or less: nothing to recover.

3 · Pick the layer the verdict reads

Push answers ±8 sd along the direction. Keep layers where the verdict moves; take the one whose direction ranks best. No labels needed.

4 · Stretch

At that one layer, multiply each answer's distance along the direction (×11). Everything else stays unchanged.

9 judges, 1B to 9B (Llama, Qwen, Phi, Mistral, Granite) 3 benchmarks: MMLU-Redux (facts), MATH (worked math), BBEH (hard reasoning) Score: ranking = how often a right answer scores above a wrong one to the same question

It works across judges: +2 to +8 points of ranking

Does it help on questions the judge can't solve?

Only right vs wrong answers written by the same model, on questions the judge itself gets wrong. That removes the easy clue "this came from the stronger model".

MATH+5.0Yes. Llama-3.1-8B, on 256 problems it failed in all 9 of its own tries (95%: +1.8 to +8.5). Even Llama-3.2-1B, which solves 16 of 602, gains +3.7.
BBEH+1.3A little. Pooled over 5 judges (+0.3 to +2.2). Granite-3.3-8B is clearest at +3.3.
MMLU≈ 0No. Pooled +0.8, interval includes 0.

A worked solution can be checked step by step without solving the problem. A recalled fact can't be checked if you don't know it.

When it fails, we can say why

Layer too early

The verdict rebuilds its decision from the answer text in later layers and overwrites the edit. Llama-3.1-8B on MATH: +1.5 at layer 14, +8.2 at layer 16.

Fixed by the push test (step 3).

Verdict reads it backwards

Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-7B distrust the most accurate answering model on MMLU, so their verdict runs against the correctness signal.

No fix at those layers.

Verdict saturated

Mistral-7B says CORRECT to 99% of MMLU answers. Pushes move its score but never flip its decision.

Fixed by measuring the score, not the yes/no.

Most of the hidden signal (75–88%) is really "which model wrote this answer". That is why gains shrink on same-model pairs.

Fresh test, rule written first
Held in 2 of 4

Two new judges, Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-7B. The 1B gains +5.4 on MMLU and +7.9 on MATH. Both misses are on BBEH, where several layers tie; the neighbouring layer gains +6.4 and +4.6.

Next: average over splits and prefer the layer the verdict reads most strongly.

Ranking gain = (share of right-over-wrong pairs ranked correctly after − before) × 100; 50% is chance. Intervals from resampling questions. Full write-up: report_2026-11-16-stretch-recipe.pdf.
