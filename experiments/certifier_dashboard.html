Dynamic LLM Juries — Team 10 × Kensho

Jury Routing Results

Five follow-up experiments on the existing judge pool (Jev, Laya, jeff, and 8 Tinker-hosted open-weight models), run on data already collected — no new API calls, no model fine-tuning. Baseline judge-vs-judge comparison lives on the Judge Agreement with Humans dashboard; this page covers what came after it.

01

Per-item certifier

A 13-parameter logistic regression — not a fine-tuned model — predicts, per item, which judge is most likely to agree with the human label, from token count, the judge's own confidence, and judge identity. Trained out-of-fold (5-fold CV, grouped by item) on data already collected.

Certifier (best K) Best single judge Unweighted majority (11) Oracle (any of 11 correct)
On BFF-noref, the certifier reaches κ=0.745 (top-3) vs. 0.705 for the best single judge — a real gain from routing alone. On BFF-ref the task is near ceiling (oracle=1.0), so the gain shrinks to 0.881 vs. 0.858. On Safety it's roughly a wash (0.640 vs. 0.637). Note: this 11-judge mixed pool is separate from the baseline dashboard's oracle, which is restricted to the 8-model Tinker pool only — the two oracle numbers aren't comparable.
02

Error diversity

Do Jev/Laya/jeff fail on different items than the 8 Tinker models, or the same ones? Phi coefficient (correctness correlation) between judge pairs — lower means more independent errors, which is exactly what makes an ensemble worth building.

Cross-family correlation (HTTP backends vs. Tinker pool) is consistently lower than intra-Tinker correlation across all three benchmarks — Jev/Laya/jeff really do make different mistakes. On Safety, they collectively catch 256 items the Tinker-8 majority gets wrong.
03

Cross-benchmark transfer

The same reliability model, fit on one benchmark's full data, applied as-is to another's — does "which judge to trust" generalize across tasks, or is it task-specific?

Transfer mostly holds (within ~0.05–0.1 κ of in-domain), except BFF-ref→BFF-noref and Safety→BFF-noref, which drop further — noref looks like the hardest, most distinct condition to predict reliability for.
04

Calibration

Is each judge's confidence score (its raw p) actually meaningful, or just a binary verdict with noise on top? Brier score against the human label, lower is better.

Jev is the best-calibrated judge on BFF-ref (Brier 0.060) and competitive elsewhere; Laya is the worst-calibrated judge on every single benchmark.
05

Frozen-threshold cascade

Reproducing arXiv:2609.26550 ("JEV-as-a-Judge"): freeze a confidence threshold on a held-out tuning split, then apply it on a disjoint test split — accept the fast judge's verdict when confident, escalate to gpt-oss-120b when not.

All three cascades land within ~1–3% of the comparator's accuracy — but coverage tells the real story: Jev's frozen threshold trusts itself on 43–97% of items and only escalates the hard ones, while Laya and jeff escalate almost everything (≤4% coverage on two of three benchmarks). Only Jev's confidence is calibrated enough to make the cascade actually save cost.
Built from already-collected judge output — experiments/certifier/ and experiments/trust_or_escalate/run_frozen_cascade.py in the project repo. No new API calls, no fine-tuning.
