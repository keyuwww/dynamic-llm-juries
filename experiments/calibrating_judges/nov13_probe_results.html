Activation Probes for LLM Judges

Context: in a dynamic jury, a cheap judge grades every answer first, and only verdicts that look doubtful are sent to a stronger judge (escalation). The open question is how to identify which verdicts to doubt. This work reads the cheap judge's activations (its internal numbers while it writes the verdict) with a small trained probe.

Probe · one linear layer on the judge's activations at the last token, right after it writes its verdict. Trained on human labels to output "how likely is this verdict right". Always tested on questions it never saw (5 folds split by question).
Accuracy at 20% · send the 20% least-trusted verdicts to the stronger judge, keep the rest, then score all final verdicts against the human labels.
AUROC · how well a score separates right from wrong verdicts. 0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect.
Judges · small: Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B, gpt-oss-20b. Large (added this round): Qwen3.8-27B, gpt-oss-120b (all five are self-hosted, so their activations are accessible).
Benchmarks · BFF (459 finance answers), Safety (RewardBench 2, 1,800), JudgeBench (620 pairs). Human labels for all.
Stronger judge · BFF/Safety: majority vote of the 3 best other judges in the 8-judge pool. JudgeBench: one other hosted judge.

3 · Most escalations do not change the verdict

Solid finding
Method
For the 20% of items escalated by the probe, the stronger judge's verdict was compared with the cheap judge's. Verdicts are yes/no or A/B, so they either agree (no change) or one of them is right.
Fixes itBreaks itBoth right (false alarm)Both wrong (no judge can help)
Result
In 8 of 9 settings, 57–76% of escalations change nothing. On BFF it is mostly false alarms. On Safety and JudgeBench, a third to half are items where the strong judge is wrong too: judges fail on the same items. "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes" (NeurIPS 2026) reports the same effect.

4 · Gain-targeted escalation

Negative
Motivation
Since most of the escalation budget is spent on items where the verdict does not change, the probe was trained on "would escalating fix this?" (strong right − cheap right) instead of "is the cheap judge wrong?".
Method
Same activations, folds and 5 seeds as the README probe. Pre-set test: new probe minus current probe at 20% escalated, with 95% confidence intervals over questions.

Explanation
On the cheap judge's mistakes, its activations predict whether the strong judge is right at coin-flip level (AUROC 0.46–0.54). On BFF it is reversed for Qwen (0.26 and 0.37): the cheap judge's confident mistakes are exactly the strong judge's mistakes.

7 · Flipping verdicts instead of escalating

Works on Safety only
Motivation
Verdicts are yes/no or A/B, so if the judge is wrong, the opposite is right. When the probe is confident a verdict is wrong, flipping it is free and also fixes the shared errors no stronger judge can fix.
Method
Probe scores were converted into a probability of error, calibrated on training questions only, and verdicts were flipped when it exceeded 50%. A combined rule chooses per item between keeping, flipping and escalating. Pre-set criterion: the combined rule must beat escalate-only in at least 2 of 9 settings.
Cheap judge aloneEscalate 20% to strong judgeFlip only (no strong judge)
Result
Qwen 9B on Safety: flipping alone gives 91.1%, above escalating 20% (88.7%) and the strong judge alone (84.1%), at zero cost. The combined rule passed narrowly: better in 2 of 9 settings (Qwen on Safety, about +1 point), worse in 4 (mostly BFF and JudgeBench).
Caveat
On BFF flipping hurts (Qwen 4B 76.0% → 73.5%): the probe is not confident enough there. An earlier run contained a calibration error; the results shown are from the corrected run.

8 · Removing the verdict direction for transfer

Helps Qwen on new benchmarks
Motivation
The probe performed at chance on benchmarks it was not trained on. Card 2 suggests a cause: it partly learns which verdict is risky on a given benchmark, and this reverses between benchmarks.
Method
The verdict direction (mean activation for one verdict minus the other; requires no labels) was removed. Probes were trained on two benchmarks and tested on the third. Pre-set bar: AUROC at least 0.60 on 2 of 3 held-out benchmarks.

Significance

Takeaways and next steps

  • The judge's activations carry a real signal about its own errors, but on Safety much of it is the verdict itself, and on BFF it is weak. Bigger judges are not easier to probe.
  • Escalation hits a ceiling because judges fail on the same items. Another LLM judge cannot fix those.
  • Where the probe is confident (Qwen on Safety), flipping the verdict for free beats paying a stronger judge.
  • Next: follow up card 8 (verdict removed, transfer to new benchmarks), add the verdict-only baseline to the README, and test the multi-layer probe on gpt-oss with the verdict removed.

All probes: the paper's linear probe and training settings, 5 folds split by question, 2–5 seeds. Pass/fail criteria were fixed before each test was run.
