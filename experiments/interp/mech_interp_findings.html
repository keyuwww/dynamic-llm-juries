Why does a detailed rubric hurt gpt-oss‑20b?

A logit-lens and causal-activation-patching investigation on BFF-Bench, following the method of Wang et al. 2025, “When Truth Is Overridden: Internal Origins of Sycophancy”.

Summary. A teammate observed that grading BFF-Bench with a detailed expert rubric raises agreement with human labels for most judges, but lowers it for gpt-oss‑20b specifically. We confirm this behaviorally using the benchmark's actual human-annotator rubric (not one we wrote): gpt-oss‑20b's κ drops 0.667→0.617, and removing a single rule — the solution steps must match the reference, not just the final answer — fully restores it (0.666). gpt-oss‑120b is unaffected. We then probe a mechanistic cause using logit lens and causal activation patching, and find a late, highly localized, near switch-like computation in gpt-oss‑20b (critical layer ≈22/24): patching three layers there recovers the correct verdict 81% of the time. This mechanism, however, is demonstrated on a different, stricter rubric we constructed to probe it, not yet on the real rubric — see §5.

1. Two rubrics

A rubric was first constructed from scratch to probe this effect. It turned out to differ from the rubric that actually produced BFF-Bench's human labels — Appendix A.4 of Setlur et al., “No Free Labels”. Both are reported below, attributed correctly, since the mechanistic result in §4 only holds for one of them.

RuleOursAppendix A.4
Final answerMatch within ~1% toleranceMust match the reference

Ours is strict on six compounding axes; Appendix A.4 is strict on exactly one (process must match) and explicitly lenient everywhere else.

3. Layer-wise decision tracking

At every layer, the hidden state is read through the model's own final norm and unembedding (the logit lens) to see which verdict the model would commit to if it stopped there. We compute a Decision Score exactly as in Eq. 1 of the source paper, restricted to the two forced-choice tokens CORRECT / INCORRECT:

DS = (logitCORRECT − min) / (max − min + ε)

A score above 0.5 means the layer currently favors CORRECT; below 0.5 favors INCORRECT.

4. Causal activation patching

Correlation at the critical layer is not causation. For every item whose verdict flips between the two prompts, we swap the hidden state at that item's own critical layer (or a 3-layer window centered on it) between the two conditions and re-run the model, following the suppression/induction design of Figure 6 in the source paper.

5. Scope of these findings

What is not yet shown: a mechanism for the real Appendix A.4 rubric's effect on gpt-oss‑20b. The logit-lens/patching pipeline reads a single forced-choice token with no reasoning allowed; under that setup the real rubric produces almost no verdict change (1 of 459 items) — too few to patch. §4's mechanism is demonstrated for our researcher-written rubric, a behaviorally different, broader intervention. The real rubric's one strict rule (steps must match) plausibly requires step-by-step reasoning to evaluate, which a single forward pass cannot perform — reproducing this for the real rubric likely needs the model to reason first, then be read at the final token. Not yet done.
