Does the model know better than it says?

Held-out validation across the full 7-model pool, plus an overnight deep-dive into why the effect is strong and stable in two models and absent or reversed in the rest.

Across 7 models × 3 benchmarks × standard/rubric, the "interior layer beats the model's own final answer" effect is real but not universal. It holds up consistently in exactly two models — everywhere else it's absent, flips with the rubric, or runs the other way.

gpt-oss-120b and qwen3.5-4b are the two models where the effect is real and consistent — large gaps, p<10-4, across almost every benchmark and condition.

Everywhere else (nemotron3-nano-30b, qwen3.6-35b-a3b, nemotron3-super-120b, gpt-oss-20b, qwen3.5-9b) it's inconsistent, negligible, or reversed — qwen3.5-9b is the clean opposite case: the final answer beats the interior layer almost everywhere.

Rubric doesn't systematically help or hurt either: even within just the two models that show the effect, rubric strengthens it in some benchmarks and weakens it in others. There's no clean "rubric worsens it" story to tell.

Overnight deep-dive

Why is gpt-oss-120b's winning layer unstable?

gpt-oss-120b's best-performing interior layer jumps between 11% and 94% of model depth depending on benchmark and condition. qwen3.5-4b's sits in a tight, stable 78–97% band every time. Two independent checks explain the difference.

1. Confidence-gap check (existing pilot data, no new compute)

Per item, the gap between the best layer's logit-lens confidence and the second-best layer's:

ModelCellTop1–top2 gap
gpt-oss-120bBFF-standard0.0034
gpt-oss-120bBFF-rubric0.0044
gpt-oss-120bBiGGen-standard0.0027
qwen3.5-4bBFF-standard0.0213
qwen3.5-4bBFF-rubric0.0311

gpt-oss-120b's gap is 7–9× smaller. Its confidence is essentially flat across most of the network — there's no real single winner, so which exact layer gets crowned "modal winning layer" is highly sensitive to sampling noise. qwen3.5-4b has a genuine, well-separated peak, which is why its winning layer is stable.

2. Jacobian lens (new GPU capture, 25–40 items/model, BFF-standard)

  • qwen3.5-4b: clean gradient flow through all 33 layers, with a real mid-network sensitivity plateau (layers 6–19, ~19–59% depth) — matches the confidence-peak finding above. Two independent signals pointing at the same mechanism.
  • qwen3.5-9b (the final-answer-wins model): smooth, monotonic decay, no mid-network feature anywhere.
  • gpt-oss-120b: gradient flow breaks completely at layer 19 of 36, for every item tested — a tooling limitation (its MXFP4-quantized MoE kernels likely lack backward support past that depth), not a finding about its representations. Jacobian lens currently can't see the back half of this model.

We are not claiming this is an MoE-architecture story — the kernel limitation means that attribution can't be confirmed yet. It stays an open question. The confirmed part is representational: gpt-oss-120b keeps a near-flat, undifferentiated confidence profile across most of its depth, where qwen3.5-4b has a real, sharp checkpoint.

Is the interior-layer signal real hidden knowledge?

On qwen3.5-9b's one large final-wins cell (BiGGen-rubric): the interior layer rescues only 2.2% of items where it disagrees with the final answer, versus the final answer winning 82% of disagreements. Close to a null result for "hidden knowledge" in this model — there's very little to rescue.

A naive "does the interior layer ever cross 0.5 on the correct side, at any layer" check was tried and discarded — with ~33 layers and a binary threshold, crossing 0.5 somewhere at least once is close to guaranteed by chance regardless of signal.

Recommended next steps, in order

  1. 1Tuned Lens on gpt-oss-120b's two genuinely-early cells (BFF-standard L10/37, BiGGen-rubric L4/37). Needs a new capture of the full final-layer softmax, not just the 2-way slice already saved. This is the main way to tell whether the early-layer "interior wins" result is real or a raw-logit-lens artifact — exactly where the near-tie above means the pick is least meaningful.
  2. 2Scale the Jacobian/probe capture from 25–40 items to ~150–300 per model — enough to train a non-overfit linear probe and get a trustworthy accuracy curve.
  3. 3Investigate the gpt-oss-120b gradient break at layer 19/36 — check whether a dequantized load path restores backward support, which would let Jacobian lens cover the model's full depth.
  4. 4Lexical-shortcut check on the layer-rescues bucket — join prompt text to see if rescued items share a surface feature (length, specific keywords), to rule out pattern-matching rather than genuine reasoning.
  5. 5Patchscopes, scoped to 6–10 hand-picked qualitative examples for the write-up, not a large run.
